## 2. RFC-2026-026: what it decides and what it leaves open

**Decides (as a proposal):**

- Two producers, one store, no trigger: the **command** (`app_command`, inside the `SECURITY DEFINER`
  command function) and the **worker** (`app_worker`); no other role inserts; no trigger writes an
  audit row (§3.1).
- Worker policy = `RFC-2026-022`'s CARRIED shape, with `causation_id is not null` as the cell's own
  predicate (§3.2).
- Command policy = actor bound to `app.jwt_subject()`, and a `succeeded` row only where
  `RFC-2026-023`'s acting-user helper admits the scope; `denied`/`failed` rows admitted for the acting
  user anywhere (§3.3). `EXECUTE` on `app.jwt_subject()` granted to `app_command` alone.
- Same transaction; an audit write that fails aborts the action; denials recorded through a PL/pgSQL
  exception block that returns a typed error rather than raising (§3.4).
- A field-by-field source table for `CTR-AUD-001` against `140`'s columns (§3.5).
- Scope checked by copying it from the changed row with `RETURNING`; denial rows carry no business or
  page unless validated (§3.6), on the producer side of `open_blockers[32]` and `[191]` (6).
- Before `DATA-DEC-03`: no producer, no interim writer, the Owner's "no audit migration before the
  worker RFC" read literally (§6).
- Tamper resistance unchanged; B adds forgery, not alteration, to the threat picture (§7).
- A coverage rule that pays for B: an audited action is performed only by a producer, and the client
  path is revoked when its command lands (§5.5).

**Leaves open:** eight questions (§4 below); the register's shape for two producers on one key; the
`roleScopedCompleteness` treatment of the command policy; the batch numbers inside 141's range.

## 3. RFC-2026-027: what it decides and what it leaves open

**Decides (as a proposal):**

- Admitted states `active`, `closing`; blocked states `access_blocked`, `purge_queued`, `held`,
  `purging`, `verify`, `deleted` (§2), read from `010_identity.sql:156-157` and ERD §11.4's diagram,
  and the same set `010` already gates the workspace row on. Written as an allowlist.
- `app_authz` gains `select (id, lifecycle_state) on app.workspaces` and one policy no wider than
  `authenticated`'s own `workspaces_select_active_member` (§3.1), which must never call the helpers
  (runtime recursion).
- `app.workspace_member_role` gains the join and the state conjunct; `is_active_member`, the roster
  policy and every family inherit; `021`'s invoker helpers inherit through their `is_active_member`
  conjunct; `RFC-2026-023`'s acting-user helpers must carry the same gate (§3.2).
- The family table (16 files) and the `010` residual: five `010` policies rewritten in the forward
  migration, `workspace_members_select_own_active` kept (§3.3).
- The exact replacement text for `RFC-2026-020` §5/3, §6.1/5 and §6.1/6 (§3.4).
- The forward migration's contents and the pins it moves: `run.mjs`'s `AUTHZ_*` constants and a
  function digest, `pinned-grants.json`, `catalog-snapshot.json`, `superseded.json` (at least `011`'s
  and `021`'s "exactly one policy" blocks), `authz-proofs.mjs` (§4).
- Nine isolation obligations, one per blocked state with positive controls and two negative controls

**Leaves open:** six questions (§4 below); the migration number in batch 170's range; four claims only

**A finding made while drafting, not owed to either RFC alone:** `010_identity.sql:403` grants the
client `UPDATE` of `lifecycle_state`, and `workspaces_update_owner`'s `WITH CHECK` (`:509-517`) does not
constrain the new state. An owner can therefore move a workspace from `active` straight to any of the
eight states — skipping the step-up and the recovery window §11.4 requires, and unaudited. Today the
effect is that the workspace row disappears; after RFC-2026-027 it is that every member is locked out
of every family. It is recorded as Q-026-5 / Q-027-5 rather than as a new blocker, because adding a
blocker is packaging; **A0 should decide at packaging whether it becomes one.**
