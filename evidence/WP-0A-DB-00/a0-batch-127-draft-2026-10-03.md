## 1. The table set, measured from the catalog

On a clean migrate of `84df4d4` plus 127, private cluster on 127.0.0.1:5507, the query was
`has_column_privilege('authenticated', c.oid, a.attnum, 'INSERT')` on `created_by` in every

approval_policies, approval_requests, asset_rights, assets, business_profile_versions,

That is the seventeen from the blocker, plus calendar_items and content_schedules, which matches the
count. `created_by` is nullable on all nineteen and has no default.

The tables carry **24 permissive INSERT policies** (five tables have two each). Every one of them
opens with `(created_by = ( SELECT auth.uid() AS uid)) AND ...`. None of the nineteen had a
restrictive policy naming created_by, and no policy anywhere had `created_by` in its name.

Every `*_by` column that authenticated may INSERT, measured the same way: **35**. That is created_by
on the 19 tables, updated_by on 14 (exactly `UPDATED_BY_CLOSURES`, 102 + 120), and requested_by on 2
(exactly `REQUESTER_CLOSURES`, 094 + 120). So the INSERT coverage is bounded, and it is pinned.

Authenticated holds USAGE on `app`, `public`, `information_schema` and `pg_catalog`. Both coverage
probes still read `app` only. See §5 (A1 F6 on 123 is still open).

## 5. What remains, and what needs the Owner or a reviewer

1. **The number (Owner).** See §0. 127 is in MOD-120's 115-129 range, and O1 covered 126 only.
2. **A computed client-encoding change stays outside the lexer's list (reviewer).** Examples:
   `set_config('client_' || 'encoding', ...)`, or `EXECUTE` of a computed SET in a DO body. Both
   change psql's encoding with no name spelled out. For `standard_conforming_strings` the fail-closed
   answer was a rule on the one place the two readings part. For a multibyte client encoding they
   part after **every** non-ASCII character, because psql masks the following byte(s). Measured on
   the fed sources at this head: **4,611 such places in 77 of 86 files**, mostly `§` before a digit
   in comments of integrated migrations, which are never edited. A byte rule would refuse almost the
   whole set. The limit is written beside the rule in `psql-driver.mjs` and in the README. A reviewer
   may prefer another remedy: pinning the client encoding of the fed session (PGCLIENTENCODING)
   does not stop a mid-file change, so it was not added.
3. **Schema `public` (A1 F6 on 123, still open).** Authenticated has USAGE on `public`. Neither
   coverage probe nor 127's block reads it.
4. **D1's mc failure is incidental.** It fires only on the two tables whose replacements pin a
   permissive count (081, 091). On the other 17 tables, a looser sibling passes mc and is refused
   only by the closure at runtime. That is the intended defence, but a reviewer may want a rule that
   pins every client-writable table's permissive set.
5. **The remedy-(b) alternative** (105's note): drop created_by from the client INSERT grant and let
   the database set it. Not done. It would change the grant contract on 19 tables.
6. **The three pre-127 forging cases are kept.** They are the invitation case and 091's two. They now
   overlap with the new family on their tables.
7. Blocker 186's text, the handoff and the manifest slot need A0's edit if this becomes the batch.
   They are untouched here.
