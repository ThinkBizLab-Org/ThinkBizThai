# Batch 140 — reversal probes, and the two claims a probe cannot reach

Run: `/claude/a1_audit`, 2026-09-06 into 2026-09-07, on `agent/claude/WP-0A-DB-00-batch-140`.
Toolchain: Node 24.20.0 / npm 11.19.0, `LC_ALL=C TZ=UTC`.

A green suite is not evidence until it can be made to go red for the reason it claims. Each probe
below reverses ONE assertion in the tree, runs the suite that should notice, records the first
failing test by name, and restores the file. Nothing here is committed; the probes are a script in
the run's scratchpad, and this file is the record of what it found.

## What this batch's probes had to cover that no earlier batch's did

Batch 140's controls are of three kinds, and only the first is the kind 020, 030 and 040 probed:

1. **Static assertions over the migration text and the case list.** Twenty-four of the probes below
   are these, and they are the ordinary shape.
2. **An apply-time proof that runs only against a database.** `140_audit.sql` writes a probe row as
   the migration role — which owns the table and holds `BYPASSRLS`, so it is exempt from `FORCE ROW
   LEVEL SECURITY` — and requires an UPDATE, a DELETE and a TRUNCATE each to raise `ZZ140`. **No
   probe in this file exercises that**, because this host has no psql, pg_ctl or docker. What the
   probes CAN show is that the static suite notices if the requirement is removed from the block,
   which is probe 3; whether the trigger actually fires is CI's to say, and until a CI round is
   green this batch's central claim is unproven.
3. **A negative statement about what a control does NOT do.** The migration says FORCE buys nothing
   against a role holding `BYPASSRLS` and that the trigger is tamper resistance rather than tamper
   evidence. Probe 15 shows that removing the first of those fails a test. Nothing can probe the
   truth of a negative; what is checkable is that the claim is still written down and that the
   measurement it rests on — `postgres` in `scripts/db/run.mjs`'s `KNOWN_BYPASS` — is still there.

## What the first round found, which is the point of running it

**Two probes were NOT NOTICED**, and neither was a missing rule. Both were rules that counted
where they should have named.

**1. `editor-a-cannot-read-their-own-audit-log` was renamed and nothing failed.** The test asserted
that at least five cases cover `§8.4/tenant-audit-select` and that each is refused at the grant
layer. The renamed case still carried the label, so the count stayed at five while the ONE CELL
THAT DISTINGUISHES THIS TABLE FROM EVERY OTHER — §8.4's `O` for the editor, the only cell in the
whole matrix where the reader IS the subject of the record — had stopped being asserted. A count is
not a cell. The rule now pins all five cells by case id AND by the identity each is about, plus the
row the editor's case reads, because "own rows" is a claim about a row this identity is the actor
of and pointing it at any other row asserts the viewer's `N` under the editor's name.

**2. One of four computed digests was replaced with a pasted hex literal and nothing failed.** The
test asked whether `sha256(convert_to(` appeared in the fixture; three survivors satisfied it while
the fourth column held an unverifiable constant — the exact thing the fixture catalog exists to
refuse. The rule now asserts the COUNT is four and, separately, that no pasted byte literal appears
anywhere, because a count alone would be satisfied by a fifth hashed column added tomorrow with a
literal.

Both were closed in `tests/db/identity/identity-isolation.test.mjs` and the second round is 26 of
26 noticed.

## What these probes do NOT establish

They exercise the STATIC half only, because this host has no psql, pg_ctl or docker and every live
target refuses by design. So:

- **The apply-time proof is unexercised.** `140_audit.sql` writes a probe row as the migration role
  and requires an UPDATE, a DELETE and a TRUNCATE each to raise `ZZ140`. Probe 3 shows the static
  suite notices when the requirement is deleted from the block; whether the trigger FIRES for a
  role holding `BYPASSRLS` is CI's to say, and until a CI round is green **this batch's central
  claim is unproven**. If that probe ever reports the owner was NOT refused, the correct response
  is to reopen the design, not to soften the block.
- **No isolation case has run against a database.** The twenty-six batch 140 cases are executable
  specification until `make db-rls-smoke` runs them in the postgres:17 container.
- **The two negative-control entries are unproven.** Each rests on one filtered read and one
  policy-layer refused INSERT; that they FAIL with row level security disabled on their table is a
  claim about a running database.
- **Nothing here says the migration parses.** It is the first migration in this repository with a
  trigger function, a `TRUNCATE` trigger, a custom SQLSTATE and a nested-subtransaction probe, and
  none of that is checked by anything on this host.

## The claim no probe can reach, recorded so a reviewer knows it is not covered

`140_audit.sql` says that `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` buys nothing against a role holding
`BYPASSRLS`, that the trigger is the only mechanism in this schema that reaches such a role, and
that it is tamper RESISTANCE rather than tamper EVIDENCE because the owner can disable it in one
statement. Probe 15 shows that deleting the first of those sentences fails a test. **Nothing can
probe the truth of a negative.** What is checkable, and is checked, is that the claim is still
written down and that the measurement it rests on — `postgres` in `scripts/db/run.mjs`'s
`KNOWN_BYPASS` — is still there, so the day `postgres` leaves that set the paragraph is out of date
and a test says so.
