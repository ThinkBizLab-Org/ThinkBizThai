### C0-256-1 (Medium): §10 records the Owner as having accepted three recommendations he was not asked about

RFC line 527–528: "**Answered 2026-10-11 UTC (2026-10-12 local).** The Owner approved this text as written (status
line), and with it A0's recommendations on Q-029-1, Q-029-5 and Q-029-7, as recommended." Disposition line 70–71
says the same thing in other words: "Q-029-1, -5 and -7 were put as A0's recommendations inside the text approved as
written, and are recorded in §10 as accepted". The PR body says "§10 records the accepted recommendations".

Neither the question nor the option he chose names Q-029-1, -5 or -7, §10, path aliases, `next-intl` or the
Q-029-7 packages. The option's description speaks only of §7, the record, the status and who merges. He declined the
option to have the RFC summarised in Thai before deciding. Approving a text "as written" approves a §10 that says
"Questions, with A0's recommendation". It does not turn those recommendations into his answers.

The RFC-2026-034 precedent shows the difference. Its §9 says "Answered" only because he answered Q-034-1 to Q-034-6
explicitly (`ยืนยันทั้ง 6 ข้อ (Recommended)`). Here, "Answered" heads a paragraph in which four of the seven questions
also stay open.

The disposition marks its other readings as A0's (§3 (b)). This one it states as fact. A later package, such as
`WP-1A-A0-002` on Q-029-1, could then cite "accepted by the Owner" for a choice he never saw. That is the "anything
new as substance" the brief asks about.

**Fix (text only):** In §10, drop "and with it A0's recommendations on Q-029-1, Q-029-5 and Q-029-7, as recommended".
Say instead that Q-029-1, -5 and -7 stay A0's recommendations inside the approved text and were not put to the Owner
as questions. Head the note "**Approved 2026-10-11 UTC**", not "Answered". Do the same in disposition §3 (a) and the
PR body. Alternatively, ask the Owner those three questions and transcribe his answer.

### C0-256-2 (Low): the disposition frames the skeleton direction as "under" RFC-2026-025 §5 item 6, which forbids it

Disposition line 77–78 says: "This is a named direction under RFC-2026-025 §5 item 6, of the kind recorded for PR
#211 and in the 2026-10-08 disposition §10.2." But §5 item 6's last bullet says a governance PR "is merged by the
Owner personally, never by delegation". RFC-2026-025's own status line and §6 call the #211 direction "an exception
to §5 item 6" (lines 8, 126). A1-251r2-6 recorded the same of §10.2.

Line 94 also says the skeleton's package, lockfile, tool configuration and `tsc` step are what "RFC-2026-025 §5 item
6 names directly". §5 item 6 names "an RFC, `CONTRIBUTING_AGENTS.md`, CI or a gate". Of the four, only the CI step is
named there. Package files, lockfiles and tool configuration are governance by RFC-2026-034 §2.2's path list, and a
new dependency by RFC-2026-029 §3.4/1.

The recorded reach is no wider than his words, so this is Low. **Fix:** call it an exception to §5 item 6's last
bullet, given in his own words for one pull request named by its sequence, as for #211. Cite RFC-2026-034 §2.2 for the
package, lockfile and tool-configuration paths.
