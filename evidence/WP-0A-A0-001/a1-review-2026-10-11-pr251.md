## 2. Security reading

The Owner chose `ทางกลาง`: drop the "Frozen before writing code" gate; ordinary PRs pass on automatic CI plus round
audits (weekly / before each gate); A1 + Q0 keep reading PRs that touch migration/RLS, auth, secret, payment. The RFC
carries that direction faithfully in §2.1–§2.3, keeps the non-negotiable rules word for word (§2.4; `CONTRIBUTING`
lines 39–47 are untouched, measured), keeps "no real customer data" and adds a `Frozen v1`-before-production line,
and is honest that it is Proposed and not in effect until the Owner merges. Its additions to the risk lane
(publishing, external side effects, CI and guards) go in the safe direction.

Three things it does go further than the option in the unsafe direction, without saying so to the Owner, and one of
them is measured to defeat the risk lane for exactly the classes the Owner kept. Those are the blocking findings.

## 3. Findings

Stop-the-line: **no**. Nothing is merged or in effect; every rule in force today stays in force until the Owner merges.

### Blocking

- **A1-251-1. CI, guards, the integrity manifest and the classifiers move out of the Owner's personal merge, and the
  RFC does not say it amends the rule that keeps them there.** RFC-2026-025 §5 item 6 (approved): "A PR that changes
  governance (an RFC, `CONTRIBUTING_AGENTS.md`, CI or a gate) is merged by the Owner personally, never by delegation";
  RFC-2026-030 §2 names "CI, a gate, or either classifier" as governance. RFC-034 §2.2 puts "CI, guards, the integrity
  manifest, either classifier" in the risk lane, pressed by A0 on the delegation after A1 + Q0 only; its governance row
  says "gate rules, the review rule itself", which also reads on the classifiers, so the table contradicts itself.
  "Amends:" names neither RFC-025 §5 item 6 nor RFC-030 §2. Q-034-3 offers the Owner only two choices, risk lane or
  fast lane, both looser than today. Under the fast lane, green CI is the **only** pre-merge control on most PRs, so
  the guards are the one thing that should stay hardest to change. **Fix:** keep CI, guards, the integrity manifest
  and both classifiers in the governance row (Owner personally, as today), or, if A0 wants the change, declare the
  amendment of RFC-025 §5 item 6 and RFC-030 §2 and add "stays governance (today's rule)" to Q-034-3 as an option the
  Owner can choose.

- **A1-251-2. The lane fails open and is decided by hand, and its stated mechanical rule sends a service-role database
  client to the fast lane.** RFC-2026-030 §2: the tier "is decided by a script, fail closed, never by the Author's
  judgement … nobody may lower it". The new "Separation of duties" passage drops "decided by the base's copy of
  `scripts/db/classify-review-tier.mjs` and never lowered by hand" and "anything the classifier cannot place"; §2.2
  is "decided by the paths a PR changes", its fast lane is "everything else", and until §8 item 2 A0 records the lane
  itself, then presses its own fast-lane PR (§3). §4 gives the rule: tier H PRs "whose only H reasons are 'a module not
  on the allowlist'…" become fast-lane. **Measured:** the classifier returns the allowlist reason first and alone. With
  `posts` not allowlisted (today every module), an added `createClient(url, process.env.SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY)` in
  `src/modules/posts/repository.ts` prints only "module posts is not on the reviewed module allowlist"; with `posts`
  allowlisted the same line prints "reads the environment (a secret path); names a privileged database credential;
  names a secret…". A `dangerouslySetInnerHTML` line behaves the same way. So a privileged client that bypasses RLS,
  which is tenant isolation and secrets, the Owner's own risk classes, lands in the fast lane under §4 as written,
  self-pressed, found at best a week later. The classifier's other reason for scripts, test kits and CONTRIBUTING
  alike is "not an L or M path", so §4's "scripts" and "test kits" are not mechanical either (this PR measured:
  all five such paths print that one reason). **Fix:** keep "decided by the base's classifier and never lowered by
  hand" in the passage; make an unplaceable path risk lane (fail closed); and make a PR fast-lane only when every
  changed file, classified as if its module were allowlisted, raises no H line signal and no H path word or stem
  in the Owner's four classes plus the RFC's own additions (migration/SQL/RLS/grant/definer; auth, session, tenant,
  role, identity; env, secret, token, credential, service_role, OAuth; Stripe, payment, billing, entitlement,
  webhook; publishing and provider calls). The interim lane record quotes that output verbatim. §8 item 2 then
  only prints it.

- **A1-251-3. The gate rewrite deletes the limit on binding a production schema or an external provider, and the RFC
  does not tell the Owner.** The text removed from "Current gate constraint" is not a Frozen rule: it is "Until G0
  passes, agents may work only on spikes, prototypes, fixtures, fake adapters, contract tests, and reversible foundation
  that does not bind production schema or an external provider", the counterpart of Decision Register line 373 ("ถ้า
  ไม่ผ่าน G0 อนุญาตเฉพาะ … reversible foundation ที่ไม่ผูก schema เท่านั้น"), which RFC-2026-031 §3.3 says binds every
  agent until the G0 exit closes. RFC-034 §1 item 1 describes the passage as allowing "implementation only on
  `Frozen v1` contracts"; "Amends:" names neither register line 373 nor RFC-031 §3.3; §5 says RFC-031 "stand[s]". The
  replacement text forbids real customer data and requires `Frozen` before a production deploy "that serves users",
  but nothing now stops, before that line, binding a live external-provider account or sending real external side
  effects (posts to real channels, mail or SMS to real recipients, live-mode charges), or applying schema to a
  production database that does not yet serve users. Those are the irreversible classes the Owner was told a late
  audit makes expensive, and they were not in the option he chose ("ยกเลิก gate 'ต้อง Frozen ก่อนเขียนโค้ด'").
  **Fix:** keep the clause in the new gate text (for example: "…before that line nothing binds a production database,
  a live-mode external-provider account, or sends to real third-party recipients"), or put its removal to the Owner as
  a §9 question; and name register line 373 and RFC-031 §3.3 in "Amends:" and correct §1 item 1.

### Advisory

- **A1-251-4. The risk lane drops RFC-030 tier-H classes that are `CONTRIBUTING` stop-the-line classes.** RFC-030 §3's H
  row also has "jobs, queues, schedulers, storage and deletion, provider adapters, mail and notifications", tool
  configuration, and the classifier stems for uploads, consent/privacy/PDPA, membership/invitations, accounts and
  admin, plus removed guard lines and code-injection sinks. `CONTRIBUTING` line 44 names lost jobs and irreversible
  deletion as stop-the-line. Pre-production with synthetic data the cost of a week's delay is small for most of these,
  so this is advisory; I recommend at least jobs/queues, deletion/purge and invitations/membership (a classic
  cross-tenant path) in the risk lane, or a §9 question.
- **A1-251-5. The production line covers only First-Slice contracts.** §2.1 and the gate text require `Frozen` for
  "every First-Slice contract the deploy uses"; the First Slice is thirteen shared-kernel contracts (RFC-031 §2), so
  CTR-USG-001 and every domain contract (billing, publishing) could reach production at `Candidate` forever, where
  register §5.1 ties implementation to `Frozen`. Recommend "every contract the deploy uses". Also add the first real
  customer data and the first user-serving production deploy to §2.3's audit triggers; either can come before "paid
  beta".
- **A1-251-6. Merged but Proposed.** `CONTRIBUTING` precedence item 1 needs an **Approved** newer RFC to outrank the
  register; §2.1 claims that rank while §9 is open, and with Q-034-1 unanswered the text already applies the looser
  answer (A0 presses its own PRs). Recommend the Owner answer Q-034-1..3 before he merges, or the RFC state that
  merging approves it with the answers written in §2.2.
- **A1-251-7. `CONTRIBUTING` contradicts itself after the merge.** "Verification and handoff" (line 59: Reviewer and
  Tester before any commit or push; an M/L merge pressed by a run that is not the Author's) and "Temporary manual merge
  control" (lines 69–80: Reviewer, Tester and Integration Owner evidence before a merge; "The Author never … authorizes
  their own work") are unchanged and forbid the fast lane; RFC-030 §2 and §3.1 (non-Author presser) are not in
  "Amends:". The stricter text fails closed when read, but leaves agents to pick a rule. R0's to integrate.
- **A1-251-8. Fast-lane "green CI" runs no application test today.** CI runs tests only under `test-kits/` and
  `tests/`, and every such test file must be digested in the integrity manifest, a risk-lane path. A fast-lane PR
  therefore either carries no test CI runs, or is risk lane; RFC-030's M condition (a test of the module in the diff,
  classifier-enforced) is gone. Recommend §8 owe CI execution of application tests and keep the test-in-diff condition
  for fast-lane module logic. Q0's to measure.
- **A1-251-9. A misclassified risk-lane PR found in an audit should keep RFC-030 §5 item 4's grade**: a security
  finding, A1 reading the merged change at once, stop-the-line if its class is one. §2.3 says only "a finding".
- **A1-251-10. Text.** §1 says "All fourteen First-Slice contracts are `Candidate`"; the First Slice is thirteen (RFC-031
  §2; CTR-USG-001 is out). All fourteen catalog contracts are `Candidate` (measured). C0's.
