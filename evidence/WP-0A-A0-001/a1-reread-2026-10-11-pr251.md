### Blocking

- **A1-251r-1. Q-034-2's recommended answer would put tenancy, identity, secrets and the job runtime in the fast lane,
  against an approved rule this RFC says it does not change.** RFC-2026-030 §4.2 item 6, and the comment over
  `M_ELIGIBLE_MODULES` in the classifier: "a module that owns identity, tenancy, IAM, sessions, secrets, billing,
  entitlement, publishing, connectors, uploads, jobs or provider calls is never added." RFC-034 line 65 says "Nothing about
  the classifier changes here", and §2.4 keeps "the classifier itself". Yet Q-034-2 (lines 178–181) asks the Owner to
  "name each First-Slice module in `M_ELIGIBLE_MODULES` in one governance PR", A0 recommending yes, and §2.2 (lines 77–79)
  calls that "what moves ordinary application code into the fast lane". The First Slice (RFC-2026-031 §2) is TEN, IDM,
  JOB, SEC, NTF, AUD, OBS and the rest of the shared kernel, and RFC-2026-029 puts its code in
  `src/modules/platform-kernel` (MOD-000, the job store and job runtime). **Measured:** with keys such as `ten`, `idm`,
  `sec`, `aud`, `ntf` or `platform-kernel`, no path word fires (`src/modules/platform-kernel/request-scope.ts` hits
  none), so once named, tenant-resolution or identity logic without an `env`, `service_role` or secret identifier on its
  added lines prints M and merges on green CI, pressed by its Author, read a week later. Tenant isolation and auth are the
  Owner's own kept classes ("คง A1+Q0 อ่านเฉพาะ PR ที่แตะ migration/RLS, auth, secret, payment"); an Owner's yes recorded
  in an Approved RFC would read as overriding item 6. **Fix (text):** Q-034-2 and §2.2 name only modules RFC-030 §4.2
  item 6 allows, quote its exclusion, and say that the kernel modules owning tenancy, identity, IAM, sessions, secrets,
  jobs, notifications and provider calls (TEN, IDM, SEC, JOB, NTF, `platform-kernel`) stay in the risk lane; I also
  recommend AUD and OBS (audit trail and log redaction) stay there, or that the Owner is asked.

- **A1-251r-2. The fast lane opens for `apps/web` without the Owner naming anything, and nobody now confirms the L and M
  conditions RFC-030 leaves to a reader.** RFC line 76: "Today the fast lane is empty. Every module is H until a
  governance PR names it"; line 127: "the fast lane is only tier M and L, which the classifier fails closed into". But
  RFC-030 §4.3's L paths include `apps/web/[src/]components/**`, copy catalogues, styles and raster images with no
  allowlist, and RFC-030 §2, §4.3 and §4.5 say L's "behind a feature flag" and "no data path", and M's "the test exercises
  the changed code", are **not** mechanical: the Reviewer or Tester confirms them on the head "or raises the tier",
  because the signal lists are denylists with known misses. **Measured** (base's copy, synthetic `apps/web/components/`
  files): a plain component; `window.open(props.href + props.email)`; `const g = globalThis; const f = g.fetch;
  f(props.u, { method: 'POST', body: props.d })`; and `el['inner' + 'HTML'] = props.html`: all four print `tier: L` and
  "the Reviewer confirms both in writing, or raises the tier (RFC-2026-030 §4.3)". Under §2.2 there is no Reviewer before
  the merge, and §2.3 does not give the confirmation to the audit. So when RFC-2026-029's layout lands, an XSS sink or a
  data channel out of the page can merge on green CI, Author-pressed, with nobody tasked to look for it; and the Owner is
  told the fast lane opens only by his answer to Q-034-2, which is not so. Synthetic data and the audit before the first
  production deploy bound the harm, which is why this is a text fix, not a stop. **Fix (text):** correct lines 76–79 and
  127 (L paths in `apps/web` enter the fast lane when the layout lands, with no naming); in §2.3, the round audit confirms
  L's two conditions and M's test condition for every fast-lane PR it covers, and a condition it cannot confirm is a
  misclassification under RFC-030 §5 item 4; and either put to the Owner whether `apps/web` L waits for a governance PR as
  modules do, or owe the RFC-030 §4.5 denylist misses (`window.open(`, aliased `globalThis.fetch`, computed property
  sinks) pinned in the classifier before the first fast-lane merge.

### Advisory

- **A1-251r-3. "Staging" and "production project" are not defined, and a hosted staging database is a live provider
  account.** Passage (a) allows migrations "on local and staging databases" and forbids any "live external provider
  account". A staging database on Neon or Supabase is both; this session has a database connector the Owner kept on
  (2026-10-10, fourth answer) with project-creation and SQL tools. Recommend: a staging project is one the Owner names as
  non-production, holds synthetic data only, and carries no production credential; "live external provider" means a
  provider that sends to, charges or acts on real third parties.
- **A1-251r-4. Guards CI runs, other than the integrity guard, sit in the risk lane.** Under the fast lane, CI is the only
  control before a merge, so the scripts `npm run check` runs (secret scan, `verify-branch-scope`, the role-separation
  validator, the handoff check, the test-suite contract) are its gate; RFC-030 §3 lists "guards" beside "gates". They
  stay read by A1 and Q0 before merge, so this is advisory: recommend the governance row read "CI and every script it
  runs as a guard", or a sentence saying why not.
- **A1-251r-5. §6 lets an open PR drop to a lighter lane** "if the roles agree", where §2.2 line 74 and RFC-030 §3.1 say
  a PR never drops. Recommend: such a PR is split or re-opened, and is classified on its own.
- **A1-251r-6. Text left at "two passages".** The handoff's `assumptions[1]`, `acceptance_results[0]`,
  `compatibility_impact` and `rollback_or_forward_fix` still say "two CONTRIBUTING_AGENTS.md passages" (four now), and
  the rollback cites "RFC §10" (the rollback is §11 now); `open_blockers[7]`'s first sentence still says "two", corrected only by the REVISED sentence. C0's.
