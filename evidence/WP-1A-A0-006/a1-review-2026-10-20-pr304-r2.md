## 3. Do the added checks strengthen the runner's security requirements?

Yes. Both are new obligations on the production runner, matching their sources word for word in substance:
- **(a) session check** — `scope.include[1]` requires, at connect and before `set local role` in every job
  transaction, `current_user = session_user = 'app_worker_login'` and no `pg_db_role_setting` row for the role. This is
  RFC-2026-028 line 6 (A1R-2, widened by A1-173-1) and the same predicate as `scripts/db/authz-proofs.mjs`'s
  `workerSessionStartProblem` (lines 1102-1108). It closes A1-173-1's path (the login role setting its own default
  `role`) and a session started under another identity.
- **(b) §5/13** — refuse a job transaction whose `current_setting('app.workspace_id', true)` is neither null nor empty
  after `set local role app_worker` and before its own `set_config(..., true)`: RFC-2026-028 lines 367-375, verbatim
  in substance. It closes the carried-tenant leak between consecutive jobs on one connection.
- `acceptance_criteria[1]` requires both **through the production runner's code path, not a harness copy**, each with
  a negative control and a check-removed red. This is stricter than the harness obligation alone and is what
  RFC-2026-029 §2.7/3 (lines 132-134) asks. Citations checked against the files at the head: correct.

Nothing is weakened: the prior sentences of `scope.include[1]` and `acceptance_criteria[1]` (SET LOCAL ROLE only,
42501 before it, no session-level SET/RESET ROLE, broker claim, D6 caps, read-once) are unchanged byte for byte.

`required_human_authorities` and `status` are unchanged. The appended `open_blockers` entry is a record only (Q-029-2
owed here; the `KERNEL_CREATED` name) and edits no other package or RFC.
