## 3. Testability of the added criterion (Q0's reading; C0 judges the RFC fit)

Both added checks have a test a tester can run on the container through the production runner, and each has a
control that must go red: (a) the bare login starts, a `pg_db_role_setting` row for `app_worker_login` is refused
at connect and at the next transaction start (the count is read live, so an existing connection sees it), a runner
with the check removed goes red; (b) two consecutive jobs on one connection, `set_config(..., false)` in the first,
the second refuses, a runner without the check goes red. The harness already decides the same cases with
`workerSessionStartProblem` and `WORKER_SESSION_READ` (`scripts/db/authz-proofs.mjs` lines 1100-1108, 1345-1357), so
the expected verdicts are measurable today. The cited lines hold on this head: RFC-2026-029 lines 132-134 (§2.7/3,
"carries the session and start-of-transaction checks ... A1R-2"), RFC-2026-028 line 6 ("A1R-2, recorded here as an obligation", for the PRODUCTION runner, and A1-173-1 in the
implemented-in-part note) and lines 367-375 (§5/13).

**Q0-304r2-I1 (Info, backlog; not blocking).** Criterion [1](a)'s negative-control example "one under `set session
authorization`" only goes red when the target role is not `app_worker_login`: under `set session authorization
app_worker_login` both `session_user` and `current_user` become the login role (the harness relies on exactly this,
`authz-proofs.mjs` lines 1049-1052), so the specified check passes it. The sentence's qualifier ("a session whose
current_user or session_user is not app_worker_login") makes the control testable as written; the words "to a role
other than app_worker_login" would remove the misreading when the increment's tests are written. The check cannot
tell a superuser impersonating the login role from the login itself; that limit is RFC-2026-028's, not this fix's.
