## Blocking

- **C0-304-1 (blocking, manifest vs RFC-2026-029 §2.7/3 and RFC-2026-028 A1R-2).** RFC-2026-029 §2.7/3 (lines 132-134)
  says the dispatcher, connecting as `app_worker_login`, "carries the session and start-of-transaction checks
  `RFC-2026-028` records as A1R-2". RFC-2026-028's implementation line (line 6) defines A1R-2 as an obligation on the
  PRODUCTION worker's runner, not only the test harness: (i) at connect and before `set local role` in every job
  transaction, refuse unless `current_user = session_user = 'app_worker_login'` and `pg_db_role_setting` holds no row
  for the role (A1-173-1); (ii) the §5/13 start-of-transaction check, refusing a job transaction whose
  `current_setting('app.workspace_id', true)` is neither null nor empty after `set local role app_worker`. This
  package is the one that writes that runner (scope.include[1] job-store transactions, [2] the dispatcher), yet
  neither scope.include nor any acceptance criterion names A1R-2, `session_user`, `pg_db_role_setting` or §5/13
  (grep over the manifest, disposition and handoff: zero hits), and open_blockers does not carry it as owed.
  Acceptance criterion [1] pins the transaction form (42501 before `SET LOCAL ROLE`; no session-level `SET`/`RESET
  ROLE`) but not these two refusals, so the package could reach `integration_verified` without a check the approved
  RFC requires. **Fix (either):** add both checks to scope.include[1] and to acceptance criterion [1] (each with its
  negative control: a session-level `set_config(…, false)` leaking into the next job; a session where `current_user`
  differs or a `pg_db_role_setting` row exists), or record them in open_blockers as a duty the first adapter
  increment's manifest amendment adds to its criteria. Either is a manifest-only edit on this branch.
